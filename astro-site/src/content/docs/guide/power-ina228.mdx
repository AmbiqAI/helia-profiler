## Purpose

Measure aggregate energy without a bench instrument. A TI INA228
current and power monitor wired into the target rail and onto the target's
I2C bus integrates energy and charge in hardware while the firmware
brackets a fixed number of inferences. The host does nothing inside the
window, so nothing the host does can contaminate it.

## Prerequisites

An INA228 carrier (a MikroE Power Monitor Click or an Adafruit INA228
breakout), a sense resistor if the carrier has none, a broken supply rail
to insert it into, and a free IOM on the board. `power.driver: ina228` is
registered in `src/helia_profiler/power/__init__.py`
(`Ina228Driver`, `supports_firmware_measurement = True`,
`supports_gated_capture = False`).

## Which carrier

| Carrier | Preset (`power.ina228.board`) | Address | Shunt | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MikroE Power Monitor Click | `mikroe-power-monitor-click` | `0x4A` as shipped (both `ADDR SEL` jumpers in the Down position); the preset sets it | none on the board; you supply and declare it | The only resistors on the board are the LED, I2C pull-ups and the ALERT pull-up (MikroE schematic); `IN+` and `IN-` go straight to a screw terminal |
| Adafruit INA228 breakout (5832) | `adafruit-ina228` | default strapping | 15 mΩ, 0.1%, on the board | The preset alone is a complete configuration; the shunt is sized for amps, see below |
| Anything else | omit `board` | set `i2c_address` if strapped away from `0x40` | set `shunt_ohms` | `shunt_ohms` has no bare default on purpose: a wrong calibration produces plausible but wrong energy |

`power.driver` names the chip the firmware talks to; `power.ina228.board`
names the carrier and fills in the facts the carrier fixes. Explicit
values win over the preset.

## Steps

### 1. Configure it

```yaml
power:
  enabled: true
  driver: ina228
  mode: internal            # the target measures itself
  ina228:
    board: mikroe-power-monitor-click   # fills i2c_address 0x4A
    shunt_ohms: 0.5         # required for this board: the resistor you wired
    max_current_a: 0.05     # size to your real peak, not the shunt rating
    i2c_iom: 1              # the Ambiq IOM wired to the monitor; its pads are in the board's am_bsp_pins.h
    # conversion_time_us: 540   # 50 | 84 | 150 | 280 | 540 | 1052 | 2074 | 4120
    # averaging_count: 16       # 1 | 4 | 16 | 64 | 128 | 256 | 512 | 1024
```

With the Adafruit board the preset is the whole block:

```yaml
  ina228:
    board: adafruit-ina228
```

The measurement runs as: reset the accumulators, run N inferences, read
energy and charge, report. All I2C traffic is outside the measured region.

### 2. Wire the Click board

| Terminal | Screw | Net | Wire to |
|---|---|---|---|
| IN1 | 1 | `IN+` | supply side of the broken rail |
| IN1 | 2 | `IN-` | target side of the broken rail |
| IN2 | 1 | `VBUS` | the target-side rail node (same node as `IN-`) |
| IN2 | 2 | `GND` | common ground |

The sense resistor goes across IN1, in series with the rail: break the
target's supply, run the supply into `IN+` and the target into `IN-`, and
the resistor bridges them. Tie `VBUS` to the target side so the chip's
power (`VBUS x CURRENT`) reports what the target received and excludes the
shunt's own dissipation. `GND` must be common with both. The INA228 is
powered from mikroBUS `VCC`, independent of the rail under test, and
tolerates a common-mode voltage up to 85 V, so a 1.8 V or 3.3 V rail is
well inside range.

### 3. Choose the shunt

Two pressures: a larger resistor gives more signal and shrinks the chip's
input offset relative to it; a smaller one steals less of the supply. Pick
the largest value whose worst-case drop still fits the high-resolution
±40.96 mV range with headroom for peaks:

| Peak current | Largest shunt inside ±40.96 mV | Burden at that peak |
|---|---|---|
| 50 mA | 0.8192 Ω (use 0.75 Ω) | up to 41 mV |
| 100 mA | 0.4096 Ω (use 0.39 Ω) | up to 41 mV |
| 400 mA | 0.1024 Ω (use 0.10 Ω) | up to 41 mV |

The limit is exact: hpx selects the high-resolution range only when
`shunt_ohms x max_current_a <= 0.04096`, so a value at the boundary lands
on the wide ±163.84 mV range and loses the 4x resolution. Round down to a
standard value. Setting `max_current_a` far above the real peak also costs
resolution.

For a target drawing about 20 mA with peaks under about 80 mA, 0.5 Ω is a
good choice: 10 mV of burden at 20 mA, about 128k ADC counts of
resolution, and offset error well under 0.05%. Dissipation is negligible
at these currents, so any 0603 or 0805 part works; buy tolerance rather
than rating, because the resistor's tolerance passes straight into the
energy figure (a 1% part means 1% energy error).

:::caution[The Adafruit board's 15 mΩ shunt is sized for amps]
A low-power target develops tens of µV across 15 mΩ, while the INA228's
input offset is on the order of a µV (datasheet `V_OS`, per ADC range), so
the offset alone is a percentage-level current error. The error is fixed
in volts and shrinks in proportion to the shunt drop: 0.5 Ω at 20 mA gives
10 mV of signal, 15 mΩ gives 300 µV, a 33x difference in how much a
microvolt of offset matters. For relative work (A/B comparisons,
regression tracking at a similar operating current) the stock board is
fine, because a stable bias cancels in the delta. For absolute milliwatts
on a sleepy target, wire your own resistor.
:::

### 4. Read the on-device summary

`summary.json` gains an `on_device_summary` block under
`measurement_scope: on_device_gated_inference`:

| Field | Unit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `energy_nj` | nanojoules | energy integrated by the chip over the window |
| `charge_nc` | nanocoulombs | charge integrated over the window; signed, clamped at zero |
| `bus_voltage_uv` | microvolts | the rail as the chip saw it; independent of the shunt |
| `inference_count` | count | inferences the firmware ran inside the window |
| `elapsed_us` | microseconds | the window as the firmware timed it |

Energy divided by count is energy per inference; average power is energy
over `elapsed_us`.

:::caution[The duration comes from the firmware, not the instrument]
Energy and charge are integrated in hardware over real time and are
robust. Average power and average current are energy or charge divided by
the window duration the firmware reports, so a firmware timing fault
scales them by exactly its own error while every other field looks
healthy. hpx cross-checks it: a zero duration is a hard error
(`power.window_clock_frozen`), and a duration more than 25% from the
host's expectation (`power.window_clock_mismatch`) or longer than the wall
time that contained it (`power.window_clock_exceeds_host_time`) is a
warning. [Verifying a capture](../power-verify/) lists them.
:::

The IOM that talks to the chip stays powered and clocked for the whole
run, on the rail being measured, so it is counted in the reported energy;
an external instrument has no equivalent cost, which is one reason the two
do not agree out of the box. The adder is the idle IOM, not bus traffic,
and it did not move between conversion-time settings on the bench that
measured it. Measure it on your own board: run once with the
`power.ina228` block and once without, using an external instrument for
both. The block's presence, not `power.driver`, decides whether the
firmware brings up a monitor, so delete the block when the chip comes off
the board; a leftover block keeps costing that current. With the block
present but the chip missing, an external-instrument run logs a warning
and continues; `driver: ina228` treats a missing chip as fatal, since the
monitor is the measurement.

### 5. Bring-up without a sense resistor

Short `IN+` to `IN-` with a jumper so the target runs through the terminal
on the wiring's parasitic resistance, and label the calibration:

```yaml
    shunt_ohms: 0.5
    calibration_id: "UNCALIBRATED-parasitic-path"
```

Short the terminal rather than leave it open: floating sense inputs give a
meaningless differential and the target loses its supply. Everything but
the absolute current scale is exercised for real: I2C bring-up, the
manufacturer and device id probes, ADC configuration, the accumulator reset
and read bracketing, the terminal envelope and the host-side parse.
`bus_voltage_uv` is fully trustworthy because it does not involve the
shunt; seeing your rail voltage confirms chip, bus and read path in one
number. Current, energy and charge are scaled by `R_parasitic / shunt_ohms`
and read one to two orders of magnitude low; the `calibration_id` is what
stops those numbers being mistaken for a measurement later.

Two readings to interpret: energy exactly zero with a non-zero bus voltage
points at the calibration register (`SHUNT_CAL = 0` zeroes every
current-derived reading; the qualified baseline pins an nsx-sensors
release with that fixed); `charge_nc` zero while `energy_nj` is non-zero
means reversed polarity, because ENERGY is unsigned but CHARGE is signed
and negatives clamp to zero. Swap `IN+` and `IN-`.

## Expected output

An `on_device_summary` with a bus voltage that matches your rail, non-zero
energy and charge, and an inference count equal to the window you asked
for. Cross-instrument runs are not compared on power by `hpx compare`
(different `measurement_scope`), and energy legitimately differs between
instruments because the shunt usually sits in a different net from the
Joulescope.

## Failure modes

The monitor is brought up before any heavy setup, so a missing or
mis-wired part fails fast with a typed terminal phase:

| Phase or signal | Meaning | Look at |
|---|---|---|
| `ina228_init` | I2C bring-up, id check (manufacturer `0x5449`, device `0x228`) or configuration failed | wiring, `i2c_iom`, address strapping (`0x4A` as shipped on the Click; `0x40` fails) |
| `ina228_init` code 10 or 11 | the shunt calibration write failed or read back a different value; hpx computes `SHUNT_CAL` on the host and verifies it, because an unverified `SHUNT_CAL = 0` reports exactly zero current, power, energy and charge while bus voltage reads fine and every conversion raises `MATHOF` | driver or bus, not wiring |
| `ina228_arm` | the accumulator reset write failed right before the window | bus |
| `ina228_read` | the post-window read failed; the window ran but the measurement was lost, so the run fails rather than report nothing | bus |
| accumulator overflow | fails the run when the monitor is the measurement of record; logs a warning when it is a bystander on an external capture | shorten the window or raise `conversion_time_us` or `averaging_count` |
| zero energy and charge, or energy without charge | a dead sense path or a window shorter than one accumulator update; or reversed `IN+` and `IN-` | wiring |

## Adding other monitors and boards

A new carrier for the INA228 is one entry in `INA228_BOARD_PRESETS`: its
strapping, its shunt if it has one, and the hint to show when a fact is
missing. A new monitor chip is a new `power.driver` value: a host driver
class (subclass the internal-mode base and set
`supports_firmware_measurement = True`), a firmware partial that brackets
the fixed-N window with the chip's own primitive, and a
`HPX_POWER_MEASUREMENT_SOURCE` value; the terminal envelope, parser,
result model and report layer are chip-agnostic. Only the INA228 ships
today.
