## Purpose

Wire a Joulescope so a capture is valid on the first try: the instrument
in series with the board's supply, one to three GPIO wires for the
handshake, and the two settings (`io_voltage`, Vref) that decide whether
the instrument sees the gate at all.

## Prerequisites

A Joulescope JS110, JS220 or JS320 (`pyjoulescope_driver` ships with hpx),
the board's power input reachable on a header, a J-Link for flashing, and
on Linux the udev rule from [Install](../../getting-started/install/#5-linux-usb-permissions).

## Which Joulescope

| | JS110 | JS220 | JS320 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current range | nA to 3 A, auto-ranging | nA to 10 A, auto-ranging | nA to 10 A, auto-ranging |
| Sample rate | 250 kSPS | up to 2 MSPS | up to 2 MSPS |
| Voltage range | 0 to 15 V | 0 to 15 V | 0 to 15 V |
| GPIO threshold | fixed; `io_voltage` tells hpx how to read it | set by the Vref pin, which must be wired | set by the Vref pin, which must be wired |
| Lock-step GO command | per-output value | GPO bitmap set and clear | GPO bitmap set and clear |

All three use the same `JoulescopeDriver` in hpx and `power.driver:
joulescope` auto-detects whichever is attached; `power.serial` (or
`--power-serial`) pins one when several are connected. The instrument
figures are the manufacturer's; hpx does not restate them anywhere else.

The same structure as a list, for readers of the Markdown rendition:

- Supply: bench or adapter
- Joulescope: IN to OUT
  - INPUT0: gate
  - INPUT1: state
  - OUTPUT0: GO
  - Vref: JS220, JS320
- EVB: VBAT, GND, GPIO
- Host: two USB links

1. Disconnect every other supply from the EVB: target USB, debug USB
   power, coin cell. A second source makes the current wrong, sometimes
   negative.
2. Wire the Joulescope `IN+` and `IN-` to the bench supply or wall adapter,
   and `OUT+` and `OUT-` to the EVB's `VBAT` and `GND`, matching polarity.
3. Connect the J-Link USB for flashing and the Joulescope's own USB to the
   host.
4. Wire the GPIO lines from the table in step 2.
5. On a JS220 or JS320, connect `Vref` to the target MCU's I/O rail on the
   EVB side of the passthrough and digital ground to EVB ground. Do not
   reference the upstream supply when it differs from the GPIO voltage.

When Vref comes from the target-side rail, normal capture keeps
passthrough enabled through flash, reset, handshake and measurement;
cycling passthrough would remove both target power and the logic
reference, so it is reserved for explicit recovery. GPIO observations made
during flash and reset are discarded; only stable samples after a short
reset grace period take part in the READY, GO and gate protocol.

### 2. Wire the GPIO lines

The minimum is one wire: the board's gate GPIO into `INPUT0`. The
three-wire lock-step handshake adds a state line (device to host,
`INPUT1`) and a GO line (host to device, `OUTPUT0`). Joulescope channels
are always `INPUT0`, `INPUT1`, `OUTPUT0`; the numbers below are Apollo
GPIO pad numbers.

| Signal | Direction | Key | Joulescope channel | Index key and default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gate | device to instrument | `power.sync_gpio_pin` | `INPUT0` | `power.sync_input_index = 0` |
| State (lock-step) | device to instrument | `power.state_gpio_pin` | `INPUT1` | `power.state_input_index = 1` |
| GO (lock-step) | instrument to device | `power.go_gpio_pin` | `OUTPUT0` | `power.go_output_index = 0` |

| Board | Registered default (gate, state, GO) | Bench-verified wiring | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `apollo510_evb` | 29, 36, 14 | 29, 36, 14, verified with a JS320 | Defaults are the verified wiring |
| `apollo510b_evb` | 29, 36, 14 | 22, 23, 24 | The default pins are not readily broken out on the 510B; set 22, 23, 24 explicitly (their only BSP claim is IOM7, which the power binary never uses) |
| `apollo4p_evb`, `apollo4p_blue_kbr_evb`, `apollo4p_blue_kxr_evb` | 22, 23, 24 | AutoDeploy AP4P wiring | Blue boards also register `ble_reset_gpio_pin` (42 on KBR, 55 on KXR) |
| `apollo4l_evb`, `apollo4l_blue_evb` | 61, 23, 24 | as registered | 22 is unavailable on AP4L; the Blue board registers `ble_reset_gpio_pin` 55 |
| `apollo3p_evb`, `apollo3p_evb_cygnus` | 26, 24, 25 | as registered | 22 and 23 are the J-Link OB VCOM UART |
| `apollo330mP_evb` | 10, 0, 0 (generic fallback; state and GO disabled) | 5, 6, 7 on the J8 header, verified with a JS110 | Set all three explicitly; the shipped `configs/mlperf_tiny/*_ap330*.yaml` do |
| `apollo5b_evb`, `apollo510dL_evb`, `atomiq110_fpga_turbo` | 10, 0, 0 | none recorded | Set the pins for your wiring; these boards stay on the gate-only path until state and GO are assigned |

The registered defaults are `get_default_sync_gpio_pin`,
`get_default_state_gpio_pin` and `get_default_go_gpio_pin` in
`src/helia_profiler/platform/registry.py`; the bench-verified column is
the wiring recorded on the pages this one replaces. For the two boards
where the columns differ, set the pins explicitly rather than trust the
defaults.

:::danger[Apollo510B: avoid GPIO 47, 48 and 49]
They are on the header but double as `VDD18_SWITCH`, `VDDUSB33_SWITCH` and
`VDDUSB0P9_SWITCH`; driving them as GPIO during a measurement can toggle
supply rails. Check `am_bsp_pins.h` for your board before choosing
alternatives, and avoid whichever IOM carries an on-target power monitor
(`power.ina228.i2c_iom`; IOM1 is GPIO 8 and 9 on the 510B).
:::

### 3. Vref and `io_voltage`

The JS110's GPI thresholds are fixed; `power.io_voltage` only tells hpx
how to interpret them. The JS220 and JS320 GPIO connector carries a Vref
pin that sets both the input threshold and the output drive level, and hpx
never programs it: it must be wired. The JS220 user's guide says to connect
Vref to the supply voltage of the device under test. Left floating, the
thresholds are undefined and the usual symptom is a gate that never reads
high, so every capture degrades to "rose but did not fall" or free-run.
The instrument can fall back to an internal 3.3 V reference, which does
not match a 1.8 V rail; wire Vref to the board's GPIO rail, keep
`Vref < VUSB - 0.5 V`, and note that a connected Vref also stops the GPOs
back-powering the target.

`power.io_voltage` (default `1.8`, `DEFAULT_IO_VOLTAGE` in
`config/power.py`) must describe the same rail. It is host-side
interpretation only.

### 4. Lock-step

`power.lockstep` (default unset, which means automatic) adds the GO and
state handshake: the firmware parks in a wait state until the host
confirms its GPIO poller is armed and asserts GO, so reset latency and
host scheduling jitter cannot race the start of the window.

- It enables itself when both `state_gpio_pin` and `go_gpio_pin` are wired
  (greater than 0) and the run is a gated external capture. It is not
  family-specific: without it the firmware free-runs its window straight
  out of reset on any board.
- An explicit `true` or `false` wins. `true` with either pin at 0 is a
  configuration error.
- Internal (INA228) mode never enables it; the measurement happens inside
  the firmware, so there is no host poller to race.

| Mode | Wiring | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Gated capture | gate only (`INPUT0`) | Bring-up, or a bench without state and GO wiring. The host can miss a short window after a slow reset. |
| Lock-step capture (preferred) | gate, state and GO | Measurements you will report. The firmware waits at `READY`; the host arms the poller and asserts GO before inference begins. |

A run captured lock-stepped is not comparable with one captured
free-running: lock-step changes the rails (the state pin becomes an
output, the GO pin's input buffer is enabled, the host holds GO high into
it until the gate rises), so `hpx compare` reports
`metric.power_power_lockstep_mismatch` and omits the power deltas. Record
baselines with the wiring you will keep.

JS220 and JS320 open and close the target-power relay through
`s/i/range/mode` (`off` or `auto`); the JS110 uses its own range selector.
`hpx power-on` and the profiler's preflight set the relay to `auto`.
Releasing the host's passthrough handle does not turn target power off;
the relay stays latched until a power cycle or an explicit relay-off.

### 5. The minimal configuration

On a board whose registered pins are the verified wiring, two lines are
enough:

```yaml
target:
  board: apollo510_evb
power:
  enabled: true
```

```bash
hpx profile model.tflite --board apollo510_evb --power
```

On `apollo330mP_evb`, name the pins and, with more than one instrument
attached, the serial:

```yaml
target:
  board: apollo330mP_evb
power:
  enabled: true
  serial: "004204"
  sync_gpio_pin: 5
  state_gpio_pin: 6
  go_gpio_pin: 7
```

`power.driver` defaults to `joulescope`, `power.mode` to `external`, and
`power.firmware` to `dedicated`; [Windows and the clean window](../power-windows/)
covers what those do.

## Expected output

A run that reports `power.sync.ready_observed: true`, one gated window,
and a `gated_window_duration_ratio` near 1.0 in `summary.json`;
[Verifying a capture](../power-verify/) explains each signal. A gate that
never rises is almost always Vref, `io_voltage` or lock-step, in that
order, before it is the wire.
