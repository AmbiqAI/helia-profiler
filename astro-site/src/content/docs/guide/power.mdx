## Purpose

Measure the energy an inference costs, not just its cycles. hpx captures
current, voltage and energy around a GPIO-gated clean window: the firmware
runs a dedicated, uninstrumented inference loop and drives a sync GPIO high
for exactly that window, while an instrument integrates charge and energy
and the host reads the result. Two instruments are supported, and this
page is the map of the five pages that cover them.

## Prerequisites

A working PMU profile of the model ([First profile](../../getting-started/first-profile/)),
and one of:

- a Joulescope JS110, JS220 or JS320 in series with the board's supply, with
  at least one GPIO wire from the board to the instrument; or
- a TI INA228 current monitor on the board's own I2C bus, for whole-window
  energy without a bench instrument.

## Which instrument

| | Joulescope (`power.driver: joulescope`, `mode: external`) | INA228 (`power.driver: ina228`, `mode: internal`) |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A bench instrument in series with the supply, watching a GPIO gate | A monitor chip on the target rail, read by the firmware over I2C |
| Streaming samples | yes | no; hardware accumulators |
| Whole-window energy | yes, integrated on the instrument | yes, integrated on the chip |
| Current distribution (median, p95, p99) | yes | no |
| Host wiring | series supply plus one to three GPIO wires | none; target I2C |
| Powers and resets the target rail | yes (relay, power-cycle recovery) | no |
| Adds to the measured current | no | yes: the IOM that talks to the chip stays powered inside the measurement |
| Pick it when | You want a sample stream, a current distribution, or a number to publish | You want cheap aggregate energy on a bench without a Joulescope, or relative A/B numbers |

`power.driver` names the chip or instrument the firmware and host talk to.
Both `joulescope` and `ina228` are registered drivers
(`src/helia_profiler/power/__init__.py`); `ondevice` is also registered as
the generic on-device base but has no firmware producer, so a run that
selects it raises `PowerError`. Use `ina228` for an on-target measurement.
Runs made with different instruments carry different `measurement_scope`
values (`gpio_gated_clean_window` for the Joulescope,
`on_device_gated_inference` for the INA228), and `hpx compare` omits power
deltas between them.

The same structure as a list, for readers of the Markdown rendition:

- Power firmware: no transport
  - Warm-up
  - READY, GO: lock-step
  - Clean window: gate high
- Instrument
  - Joulescope: gate on INPUT0
  - INA228: I2C, accumulators
- Host
  - Window checks: two clocks agree
  - summary.json: power block

In order: hpx flashes a second, transport-free build of the firmware
(`power.firmware: dedicated`, the default) that does model init, warm-up,
the three-wire lock-step handshake and the gated clean window, then parks.
With a Joulescope, the host watches the gate on `INPUT0`, sums the
statistics packets that fall inside it, and divides the gated energy by
the firmware-reported clean inference count. With an INA228, the firmware
resets the chip's accumulators, runs a fixed number of inferences, reads
energy and charge back, and reports them through the same terminal record.
Either way the host cross-checks the firmware's own window clock against
the instrument before it publishes per-inference numbers.

## Expected output

`summary.json` gains a `power` block with `measurement_scope`, average and
peak current, average power, the current percentiles (Joulescope), energy
per inference, inferences per joule, and the health signals the
verification page lists; the terminal prints a compact power table. The
[power examples](../../examples/) show captured runs.
